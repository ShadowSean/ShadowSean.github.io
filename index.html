Nebula

NEBULA / SYSTEM BREAKDOWN

Camera look rotation

How the system works

The camera rotation system in Nebula uses the player's mouse input to look freely around the 3D space environment. Horizontal and vertical mouse movements are detected and converted into camera rotation using Unity's Quaternion system. Adjustable X and Y sensitivity settings control the speed of rotation on each axis. Vertical rotation is clamped between −90° and 90° to prevent excessive camera tilting, while a separate orientation transform tracks the player's horizontal facing direction to maintain consistent movement controls.

NEBULA / SYSTEM BREAKDOWN

Jumping and movement control

How the system works

The player movement system uses keyboard inputs to move the player in multiple directions relative to the direction the camera is facing. Movement is handled using Unity’s Rigidbody physics system, which applies forces to move the player around the environment. Jumping is performed by applying an upward force when the spacebar is pressed, while a ground detection system ensures the player can only jump when touching the ground. A jump cooldown also prevents continuous jumping.

NEBULA / SYSTEM BREAKDOWN

Low gravity mechanics

How the system works

The low gravity system is designed to create a space-like experience where players can jump much higher than usual and travel far distances between asteroid platforms. By adjusting the jump force and air movement multiplier, the player can jump much further while still having good control of their movement in the air. These settings allow for a floating movement experience that supports the game’s main platforming mechanics. The effect can be changed by reducing the Rigidbody’s gravity through a separate gravity configuration.
